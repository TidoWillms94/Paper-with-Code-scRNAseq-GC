# Paper-with-Code-scRNAseq-GC
Analyzing the expression level of genes of interest in single-cell RNA sequencing data from gastric cancer patients (blood, paratumor, and tumor tissue) across different cell types (Epithelial cells, Tumor cells, CD8+ T cells, CD4+ T cells, regulatory T cells, NK cells )
# Data availability
Single-cell sequencing data (.fastq files) were accessed through the Genome Sequencing Archive from the China National Center for Bioinformation (CNCB) under the accession number [HRA000704](https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/gsa-human/browse/HRA000704).  
The sequencing data were made public through the publication by K. Sun et al. in Nat. Commun 13, 4943 (2022):  
[scRNA-seq of gastric tumor shows complex intercellular interaction with an alternative T cell exhaustion trajectory]( https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-32627-z) Information for sample collection, processing, and library preparation can be accessed through the linked paper in the Methods section.


# Method
## Single cell RNA-seq data processing
The FASTQ reads from the gene expression (GEX) and V(D)J libraries were aligned to the respective reference genomes (GRCh38-2024A and VDJ-GRCh38-alts-ensembl-7.1.0) using Cell Ranger 9.0.0 (RRID:SCR_017344) and its corresponding pipelines for gene expression counting and V(D)J analysis. Further preprocessing and analysis were performed using Scanpy 1.12.4 (RRID:SCR_018139) in Python 3.13 (RRID:SCR_008394). Quality control included filtering out cells with a low UMI count (<400 UMIs), a low number of detected genes (<200 genes), or a high proportion of mitochondrial transcripts (>30%). Gene expression counts were normalized per cell to a total of 10,000 counts (scanpy.pp.normalize_total), excluding genes that accounted for more than 10% of a cell’s total counts when computing the size factors, to correct for differences in sequencing depth between cells. Normalized counts were then log₂(x+1)-transformed, and the samples were concatenated into a single AnnData object for downstream analysis. First, doublets were removed using scDblFinder 1.24.10 (RRID:SCR_022700). Additional doublets were identified using the V(D)J sequences by excluding T cells with more than two TRA or TRB chains. Cell types of interest were identified by iterative subclustering combined with quality control measurements and filtering. Tumor cells were identified using three complementary approaches: first, by calculating a tumor score as proposed by M. Zhang et al. (2021); second, by copy number variation analysis; and third, by comparing clusters with the mutation scores from K. Sun et al. (2022). After the cell clusters were annotated as epithelial cells, tumor cells, CD8⁺ T cells, CD4⁺ T cells, regulatory T cells, and NK cells, the expression of genes of interest was visualized in a dot plot.
